The week · both phases

What a week is

Twelve to fifteen hours. Four swims because frequency is what builds a stroke, three rides because the bike is the biggest chunk of race day, three runs plus two soccer nights, and four lifts because twenty-six weeks is long enough to get genuinely stronger before the race takes over.

Four lifts and hard cardio both fit — but not at full size the whole way. Strength work is cheapest now, far from the race: four lifts a week through 14 December, three from 15 December, two from 16 March while cardio volume peaks. That ordering is the only reason both fit at all.

The eating clock · timing, not just totals

When to eat

This is the timing. The actual named meal in every one of these slots, on every date, is on the Diet page and on the day itself on the Daily page.

Daily targets live on the Diet page and on every date of the Daily view. Timing is the other half: the same calories placed badly train worse and recover slower. This is the consensus position of the sports-nutrition literature, sized to you at ~73 kg.

Rules that outrank motivation

Hard lines

  1. 8 hours of sleep is a training session. The two short nights the plan tolerates (late Fridays) are scheduled, not accidents.
  2. The swim gates are dates, not vibes: 800 yd by 8 Nov, 1,500 yd by 13 Dec, 2,000 yd by 7 Feb, 1.2 mi open water by 14 Mar. Miss one → buy coaching that week.
  3. No cutting during the build. Maintenance carbs are what make four lifts and this much cardio absorbable. Cut after 4 April.
  4. Nothing new on race day — fuel, kit, pacing all rehearsed from week 5.
